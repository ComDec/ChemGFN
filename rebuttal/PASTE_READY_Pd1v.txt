We thank Reviewer Pd1v for the feedback. We address each concern below.

**W1: Experimental breadth.**

We agree that our strongest application-scale evidence is in scaffold-conditioned SMILES, and we revise the claim scope accordingly. Our intended claim is not universal gains across all LLM post-training settings, but improved training behavior on terminable prefix-tree LLM-GFlowNets in the evaluated regimes.

That said, the failure modes we target are observed beyond molecules:
- Expr24: a fully verifiable sparse-reward task where exact posterior comparison is possible. Table 3 shows RapTB+SubM achieves NormCov 0.209, doubling TB+SubM's 0.100, while maintaining near-perfect accuracy (>0.99).
- CommonGen: a realistic language diagnostic (Table 5) where SubTB exhibits extreme termination drift (Delta log p_term = -28.32, saturating length at 20.00), while RapTB maintains calibration (Delta log p_term = -0.94) and achieves superior BLEU (11.75 vs 5.95 for TB).
- SMILES L_max=15: a longer-horizon stress test (Table 2) where RapTB+SubM achieves the best balance of long-horizon coverage, quality, and diversity.

The failure modes (prefix collapse and termination drift) arise from the structure of the prefix tree and the SubTB objective, not from anything specific to SMILES. We will revise claims to make this structural argument more prominent.

In the revision, we also add an explicit averaged-prefix TB baseline (see response to Reviewer QHmk) that further demonstrates the necessity of RapTB's specific design choices.

**W2: Baseline methods.**

We chose TB and SubTB as primary baselines because our contribution is an objective-level modification within the TB family. We agree that broader baselines strengthen the paper:

- TBA [Bartoldson et al., NeurIPS 2025]: This is the most relevant system-level work. TBA is an asynchronous TB-based LLM post-training pipeline with replay, tested at 410M-7B scale. Our contribution is orthogonal: improving credit assignment within the TB objective itself. RapTB could serve as a drop-in replacement for the TB loss inside TBA. We will add explicit discussion.

- PPO/GRPO: We now include a GRPO baseline on Expr24. GRPO achieves Acc=0.002 (12/6400 valid), with 99.9% of generations collapsing to maximum length and zero oracle coverage. This confirms reward-maximizing RL cannot solve the distributional sampling problem — the exact failure mode our method addresses.

- Averaged-prefix TB: We implement and evaluate the baseline of averaging TB loss over all prefixes, as suggested by Reviewer QHmk. This directly tests whether the gains come from "any dense prefix supervision" vs. RapTB's specific design.

Additionally, we include four replay strategies (PRT, RP, SubM, Oracle) and multiple ablations: max-only, soft-only, w/o reward absorb, w/o detach pterm, and RootSubTBLogZ (Tables 3-6). These isolate the contribution of each component.

**W3: Theoretical guarantees.**

We agree that the current paper does not prove a new exact consistency theorem for the full composite RapTB objective. Our intended claim is narrower: the terminal TB term remains the only exact balance condition whose optimum matches the reward-proportional target (Eq. 9), while the rooted absorbed auxiliary branch is a variance-reducing regularizer that improves optimization in practice.

The Oracle replay experiment (Table 3) provides the strongest empirical evidence: when coverage is externally controlled, RapTB still outperforms TB in accuracy (0.945 vs 0.919), KL(pi->p*) (0.052 vs 0.062), and JS (0.013 vs 0.016). This confirms that RapTB improves internal credit assignment without distorting the target distribution.

We will revise the wording throughout to avoid any implication of a new exact guarantee.
