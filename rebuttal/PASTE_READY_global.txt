We thank all reviewers for the detailed and constructive feedback. We address three overarching points before responding individually.

**1. Theoretical scope of RapTB (Pd1v-W3, JxzD-W1, QHmk-Q1).** We clarify that RapTB does not claim a new exact fixed-point theorem. As stated in Eq. (9), the terminal TB term remains the only exact balance condition whose global optimum matches the reward-proportional target. The rooted absorbed auxiliary branch is a variance-reducing regularizer that improves optimization dynamics without altering the fixed point. We will revise the text to make this distinction explicit throughout.

**2. RL contextualization and TBA (QHmk-W1/W3, Pd1v-W2).** We acknowledge that GFlowNets can be reformulated as entropy-regularized RL [Tiapkin et al., AISTATS 2024; Deleu et al., UAI 2024], and that the KL-regularized reward-proportional setting studied here fits within this broader framework. We will revise the introduction accordingly. Regarding TBA [Bartoldson et al., NeurIPS 2025]: TBA is a system-level pipeline for asynchronous TB-based LLM post-training with replay, while our contribution is orthogonal — improving within-trajectory credit assignment and termination calibration at the objective level. RapTB can in principle be used inside TBA's training loop. We will add explicit discussion of TBA and the RL reformulation in the revision.

**3. RapTB and SubM are complementary, not competing (cA3o-Q1).** SubM is the dominant mechanism for external mode discovery; RapTB improves internal credit assignment and distribution calibration. Three regimes support this: (i) Under RP replay, RapTB already improves coverage over TB (NormCov 0.039 vs 0.001 on Expr24). (ii) Under SubM, RapTB+SubM doubles NormCov over TB+SubM (0.209 vs 0.100). (iii) Under Oracle replay where coverage is controlled, RapTB still outperforms TB in accuracy (0.945 vs 0.919) and KL/JS, indicating better probability allocation once discovery is fixed. We will surface these comparisons in the main text.

**New experiments in the revision:**
- PPO/GRPO baselines (QHmk-W2): Both fail on Expr24 (N=6400). GRPO produces only 12 valid expressions (1 unique); PPO collapses within 50 steps (20 valid, entropy→0). Reward-maximizing RL cannot solve distributional sampling.
- Averaged-prefix TB baseline (QHmk-Q2): AvgPrefixTB achieves high accuracy but severe mode collapse on both Expr24 (NormCov 0.016 vs RapTB's 0.039) and SMILES (QED 0.661, Diversity 0.665 vs RapTB+SubM's 0.844/2.726). Simple prefix averaging does not match RapTB's specific design choices.
- Hyperparameter robustness (cA3o-Q2): We sweep beta x rho (9 configs each) on both Expr24 and SMILES (18 total), plus eta (3) and k_min (3) ablations on Expr24. On Expr24, all 9 configs achieve Acc >= 0.983 with stable termination calibration. On SMILES, 8/9 achieve Acc >= 0.991 with FPDiv 0.849-0.883 matching the paper (0.860). eta shows monotonic improvement; fixed-low k_min is clearly worst.
- Model scale-up (Pd1v-W1, JxzD-Q3): We scale from Llama-3.2-1B to Llama-3.2-3B on SMILES QED. SubTB's termination drift is amplified at 3B (log p_term = -25.0, Acc = 0.313), while RapTB+SubM achieves Acc=0.996, QED=0.856, FP Diversity=0.937 — the best across all methods.
- AMP biological sequence generation (JxzD-Q3, cA3o-Q3): We evaluate on antimicrobial peptide generation (20-50 amino acids, non-differentiable fitness predictor). SubTB again exhibits length collapse (Avg Len=49.3, all at max). Among methods generating natural-length sequences, RapTB+SubM achieves the best diversity (16.92) and novelty (15.77) in only 3K steps. This confirms the structural failure modes generalize to biological sequence design.

We revise claim scope from "general LLM-GFlowNets" to "terminable prefix-tree LLM-GFlowNets in the evaluated settings," consistent with the structural (not domain-specific) nature of the identified failure modes.
