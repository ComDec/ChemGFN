We thank Reviewer QHmk for the thorough review. We address each concern below.

**W1: RL contextualization.** We fully agree and will revise. The connection between GFlowNet training and entropy-regularized RL is well-established [Tiapkin et al., 2024; Deleu et al., 2024]: sampling from p*(x) ∝ P_ref(x)·exp(r(x)/β) is equivalent to KL-regularized RL. Our introduction overstates the distinction. Our contribution operates within this framework: improving training objectives within the TB loss family for high-variance credit assignment and termination drift on terminable prefix trees. However, theoretical equivalence does not imply standard RL algorithms perform identically — TB-family objectives enforce flow consistency across full trajectories, providing structural inductive bias toward distributional coverage (see W2).

**W2: PPO/GRPO baselines.** We add both on Expr24 and SMILES with identical model/LoRA/budget (unconstrained generation, soft vocab masking):

| | SMILES (dense QED) | | Expr24 (sparse) | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Method | QED↑ | Entropy↑ | Acc↑ | Entropy↑ |
| PPO | 0.604 | 0.00 | 0.000 | — |
| GRPO | 0.661 | 0.98 | 0.872 | 0.89 |
| TB | 0.717 | 2.503 | 1.000 | — |
| RapTB+SubM | **0.844** | **2.726** | **0.991** | **1.208** |

PPO collapses entirely (entropy=0 on SMILES, Acc=0 on Expr24). GRPO learns reasonable reward but entropy=0.98 — only 36% of GFlowNet's diversity on SMILES. The diversity gap persists on **dense-reward** SMILES, confirming a fundamental difference between reward-maximizing and distributional objectives, not reward sparsity. Consistent with Hu et al. (2024).

**W3: TBA baseline.** TBA uses TB-VarGrad (trajectory-level balance, batch-estimated Z), with no SubTB or prefix-level credit. TBA's contribution is orthogonal — scalable asynchronous infrastructure (410M-7B, 4-50× speedup). Ours addresses credit assignment within TB. TBA explicitly acknowledges TB's gradient variance as a limitation and suggests "learning partial energy functions" as future work — RapTB's absorbed suffix backups directly address this. RapTB is a drop-in replacement for TBA's TB loss. We did not run TBA experiments because (1) it requires multi-node distributed infrastructure, and (2) the loss comparison is covered by our TB baseline.

**W4/W5: Mathematical explanation.** RapTB = TB + auxiliary regularizer (Eq. 9): the TB anchor is the sole exact balance condition; the auxiliary term enhances credit assignment via three designs: (1) Rooted residuals (Eq. 4) cancel log Z in the auxiliary branch, creating a root-anchored local signal without O(τ) redundant Z optimization — the global Z is still learned through TB. (2) Absorbed suffix backups (Eqs. 5-7) aggregate downstream reward evidence into lower-variance prefix targets, replacing the high-variance terminal-only signal. (3) Stop-gradient on the termination head in the auxiliary branch (Eq. 27) directly prevents the termination drift diagnosed in Appendix C.6. We will define "termination drift" at first mention.

**Q1: Global optimum.** No new exact guarantee is claimed for the composite objective. The reward-proportional fixed point is tied to the TB anchor. The auxiliary term introduces optimization bias, not a distortion of the converged solution — at the TB fixed point (Δ^TB=0), rooted residuals also vanish, so L_aux=0. In principle, annealing η→0 recovers exact TB. Empirically, RapTB achieves better distributional fidelity than TB (Table 3: JS 0.147 vs 0.339).

**Q2: AvgPrefixTB.** We implement exactly this: averaging (Δ_k^TB)² over all k with learnable Z.

SMILES (L_max=10):

| Method | Acc | QED↑ | Entropy↑ | FPDiv↑ | Avg Len |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TB | 0.998 | 0.717 | 2.503 | 0.807 | 3.06 |
| AvgPrefixTB | 1.000 | 0.661 | 0.665 | 0.649 | 2.89 |
| RapTB | 0.996 | 0.740 | 2.448 | 0.860 | 6.14 |
| RapTB+SubM | 0.988 | **0.844** | **2.726** | **0.898** | 7.44 |

Expr24 (RP replay, 6400 samples):

| Method | Acc | Unique↑ | NormCov↑ | JS↓ | Avg Len |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TB | 1.000 | 5.3 | 0.001 | 0.339 | 8.98 |
| SubTB | 0.229 | 324.7 | 0.051 | 0.109 | 8.09 |
| AvgPrefixTB | 0.998 | 142 | 0.016 | 0.213 | 5.74 |
| RapTB | 0.991 | 246.7 | 0.039 | 0.147 | 8.99 |

On SMILES, AvgPrefixTB is the **worst** method — lower QED, diversity, and FPDiv than vanilla TB. Avg Len 2.89 (54% at L=1-2): short sequences trivially satisfy all prefix constraints. On Expr24, AvgPrefixTB similarly collapses to short sequences (Avg Len 5.74 vs TB/RapTB's ~9.0), with only 142 unique valid (vs RapTB's 247). Both tasks show the same pattern: simple prefix averaging creates a shortcut favoring short, easy-to-satisfy sequences. RapTB avoids this through (1) rooted residuals eliminating redundant Z pressure, (2) absorbed backups reducing early-prefix variance, and (3) stop-gradient preventing termination drift.
