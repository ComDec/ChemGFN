**Q1: When does RapTB provide additive benefit over SubM alone?**

We clarify the role separation with three controlled comparisons:

(1) *Under RP replay (no SubM)*: RapTB already improves coverage over TB. On Expr24, NormCov increases from 0.001 (TB) to 0.039 (RapTB), and Unique_valid from 5.3 to 246.7. SubM is not needed for RapTB to show gains.

(2) *Under SubM*: RapTB+SubM further improves over TB+SubM. NormCov doubles on Expr24 (0.209 vs 0.100). This is the additive benefit.

(3) *Under Oracle replay* (Table 3, coverage controlled): RapTB outperforms TB in accuracy (0.945 vs 0.919) and both KL directions, indicating better probability mass allocation when discovery is fixed. This is the cleanest evidence that RapTB's gain is internal calibration, not accidental mode discovery.

On SMILES (Appendix A.1, Table 7), TB+SubM and RapTB+SubM achieve similar average Score (~0.844), consistent with a ceiling effect. However, RapTB+SubM allocates more mass to the longest length bin (Frac[9-10]: 0.402 vs 0.323), consistent with improved suffix credit assignment.

We will surface these comparisons in the main text and make the SubM=external/RapTB=internal distinction explicit.

**Q2: Hyperparameter sensitivity.**

We conduct a systematic robustness study on Expr24 under RP replay:

*beta x rho sweep (9 configs, 5000 steps, Expr24 RP)*: We sweep beta in {1,3,5} x rho in {0, 0.1, 0.5}. Results (see heatmap in revised PDF):

- Accuracy is uniformly robust: all 9 cells achieve Acc >= 0.994. No catastrophic failure at any point, confirming the method is not a fragile sharp optimum.
- Termination calibration is stable: log p_term(tau) in [-0.25, -0.04] across the grid, orders of magnitude better than SubTB's -79.6 (Table 4).
- Distributional fidelity (JS_tok) ranges 0.179-0.243; several configs match or improve the paper default (0.228), showing no narrow optimum.

*eta sweep (3 points, beta=3, rho=0.5)*: eta in {0.1, 0.25, 0.5} shows monotonic improvement: higher auxiliary weight consistently improves NormCov (0.008 -> 0.012 -> 0.014), KL(pi->p*) (0.807 -> 0.668 -> 0.631), and JS (0.235 -> 0.195 -> 0.185) at negligible accuracy cost. eta is not a nuisance parameter — it has clear, interpretable behavior.

*k_min schedule ablation (3 variants)*: Fixed-low (k_min=3) is clearly worst: Acc drops to 0.990, NormCov halves, log p_term degrades to -0.38. Schedule (7->3) and fixed-high (k=7) perform comparably. This validates the design rationale: early short prefixes carry noise and should be excluded initially.

Two existing defenses: (1) eta=0.25 and gamma=0.99 are shared unchanged between SMILES and Expr24 (Table 23), so not all parameters require task-specific tuning. (2) Table 6 already ablates max-only vs soft-only, showing mixed is preferred.

**Q3: Domain generalization.**

We agree that the strongest application-scale evidence is in SMILES. Our failure modes (prefix collapse, termination drift) are structural to terminable prefix trees, not domain-specific — they appear across all three tasks (SMILES, Expr24, CommonGen) and under L_max=15 stress testing. We revise claims to "terminable prefix-tree LLM-GFlowNets in the evaluated settings." Biological sequence generation (amino acids, nucleotides) is the most natural next domain and we plan to explore it.

We additionally validate model-scale generalization by scaling from Llama-3.2-1B to Llama-3.2-3B on SMILES QED. The 3B results confirm all conclusions hold at larger scale, and SubTB's termination drift is actually *amplified* (log p_term = -25.0, Acc drops to 0.313). RapTB+SubM achieves the best overall quality-diversity trade-off (Acc=0.996, QED=0.856, FP Diversity=0.937).

**Q4: GAE analogy.**

We view the connection as a bias-variance analogy, not an exact equivalence. GAE inserts a bootstrapped multi-step estimator into a policy-gradient objective. RapTB inserts an absorbed suffix backup into a squared rooted balance residual while keeping terminal TB as the global anchor. The key structural difference is that GFlowNet balance conditions enforce flow consistency (a squared residual loss), not policy gradient updates. The partition function Z and the balance structure create a fundamentally different optimization geometry. We agree that connecting to the entropy-regularized RL view [Tiapkin et al., 2024] would strengthen the discussion and will add this.
