**Q1: When does RapTB provide additive benefit over SubM alone?**

Three controlled comparisons clarify the role separation:

(1) *Under RP replay (no SubM)*: RapTB already improves coverage over TB. On Expr24, NormCov increases from 0.001 (TB) to 0.039 (RapTB), and Unique_valid from 5.3 to 246.7.

(2) *Under SubM*: RapTB+SubM further improves over TB+SubM. NormCov doubles on Expr24 (0.209 vs 0.100). This is the additive benefit.

(3) *Under Oracle replay* (Table 3, coverage controlled): RapTB outperforms TB in accuracy (0.945 vs 0.919) and both KL directions, indicating better probability mass allocation when discovery is fixed.

On SMILES (Appendix A.1, Table 7), RapTB+SubM allocates more mass to the longest length bin (Frac[9-10]: 0.402 vs 0.323), consistent with improved suffix credit assignment. We will make the SubM=external/RapTB=internal distinction explicit in the main text.

**Q2: Hyperparameter sensitivity.**

We conduct a comprehensive cross-task robustness study:

*Expr24 beta x rho (9 configs, bsz=128, 5000 steps, N=6400)*: All 9 cells achieve Acc >= 0.983 with stable termination calibration (log p_term in [-0.25, -0.04] vs SubTB's -79.6). Diversity (token entropy) ranges 0.77-1.02 across configs, confirming no catastrophic failure at any grid point.

*SMILES beta x rho (9 configs, bsz=128, 5000 steps)*: We sweep beta in {1,5,10} x rho in {0,0.1,0.5}. 8/9 configs achieve Acc >= 0.991 (only beta=10,rho=0 shows mild degradation at 0.968). FPDiv ranges 0.849-0.883 (paper: 0.860) and QED ranges 0.73-0.80 (paper: 0.740) — matching the paper across the grid. No length collapse in any config (mean length 6.7-7.7).

*eta sweep (3 points, beta=3, rho=0.5)*: eta in {0.1, 0.25, 0.5} shows monotonic diversity improvement (0.98 -> 0.98 -> 1.15) at negligible accuracy cost. eta is not a nuisance parameter — it has clear, interpretable behavior.

*k_min schedule ablation (3 variants)*: Fixed-low (k_min=3) is clearly worst (diversity 0.85 vs default 0.98). Schedule (7->3) and fixed-high (k=7) perform comparably, validating the design rationale that early short prefixes carry noise.

In total, we evaluate 18 beta x rho configs across two tasks + 6 eta/k_min ablations. The method is robust: no collapse, no termination drift, consistent quality metrics.

**Q3: Domain generalization.**

We agree that the strongest application-scale evidence is in SMILES. Our failure modes (prefix collapse, termination drift) are structural to terminable prefix trees — they appear across all three tasks (SMILES, Expr24, CommonGen) and under L_max=15 stress testing. We revise claims to "terminable prefix-tree LLM-GFlowNets in the evaluated settings."

We additionally validate model-scale generalization by scaling from Llama-3.2-1B to Llama-3.2-3B on SMILES QED. SubTB's termination drift is *amplified* at 3B (log p_term = -25.0, Acc drops to 0.313). RapTB+SubM achieves the best trade-off (Acc=0.996, QED=0.856, FPDiv=0.937).

**Q4: GAE analogy.**

The connection is a bias-variance analogy, not an exact equivalence. GAE inserts a bootstrapped multi-step estimator into a policy-gradient objective; RapTB inserts an absorbed suffix backup into a squared rooted balance residual with terminal TB as the global anchor. The partition function Z and the balance structure create a fundamentally different optimization geometry from policy gradient. We agree that connecting to the entropy-regularized RL view [Tiapkin et al., 2024] would strengthen the discussion and will add this.
