**Q1: Why do SubTB constraints include termination probabilities?**

In the terminable prefix-tree specialization (Appendix C.6), SubTB eliminates explicit state-dependent flow variables. Each arbitrary-start window residual (Eq. 33) contains a boundary difference of termination log-probabilities. Because windows overlap extensively and all share a single termination head, the optimizer faces O(N^2) heterogeneous boundary conditions on the same logits. The model can reduce aggregate squared loss by globally shifting the termination head rather than improving token-level transitions — this is termination drift.

Our RootSubTBLogZ ablation (Table 4) validates this: restricting SubTB to rooted windows and reintroducing learnable Z_theta restores accuracy to ~100% on Expr24.

Learning explicit state flow values is a reasonable conjecture that could reduce termination head pressure. It changes the parameterization substantially and falls outside the current scope, but we note it as a promising direction.

**Q2: Prefix survival metric.**

Surv(k) = n_k / n_valid (Appendix B.3), where n_k counts valid samples with length >= k. Interpret jointly with PefEnt and Top1: high Surv + high Top1 = trajectories reach depth k but share few prefixes; high Surv + low Top1 + high PefEnt = genuine early branching. Figure 3 shows TB has rapid survival decay with increasing Top1 (prefix collapse), while RapTB maintains higher survival with lower Top1.

**Q3: Longer sequences and larger models.**

Our longest-horizon evidence is SMILES L_max=15 (Table 2), where RapTB+SubM achieves the best long-horizon coverage (Frac(11+)=0.701) and lowest prefix concentration (Top1 0.071). AMP/GFP is the most natural next extension and planned as follow-up.

We scale from Llama-3.2-1B to Llama-3.2-3B on SMILES QED:

| Method | Acc | Score | Entropy | FPDiv | Len |
|--------|-----|-------|---------|-------|-----|
| TB (3B) | 0.999 | 0.717 | 1.905 | 0.837 | 2.74 |
| SubTB (3B) | 0.313 | 0.221 | 2.090 | 0.854 | 8.48 |
| RapTB (3B) | 0.984 | 0.732 | 2.252 | 0.864 | 6.86 |
| RapTB+SubM (3B) | 0.996 | 0.856 | 2.447 | 0.937 | 7.96 |

SubTB's drift *worsens* at 3B (Acc 0.313, 64% at max length). RapTB+SubM achieves the best trade-off (Score=0.856, FPDiv=0.937).

**Q4: Why fine-tune LLM?**

The frozen reference LM prior (P_ref) is part of the reward definition (Eq. 17), not an incidental choice. It anchors generation toward fluent outputs. CommonGen uses this anchor to isolate objective-induced termination drift. Training from scratch would answer a different question; we expect the failure modes would persist (they are structural to the prefix tree + SubTB objective).

**W1: Convergence analysis.**

The terminal TB term retains the exact reward-proportional fixed point. The auxiliary branch is a variance-reducing regularizer. We do not claim a new convergence theorem for the composite objective.

**W2: Absorbed suffix backup motivation.**

Terminal-only rewards provide no signal about which early decisions led to good outcomes. The absorbed backup (Eqs. 5-7) constructs lower-variance proxy credit: u_max provides an optimistic lower bound, u_soft smoothly aggregates suffix evidence with distance discounting, and alpha interpolates between them. Table 6 confirms mixed > either endpoint alone. Detaching pterm in the auxiliary branch is critical — without it, sequences collapse to Len 3.40.
